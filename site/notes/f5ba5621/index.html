Bias strips have two values

#recurrence

A bias vector has one permuted axis and no free axis. Its orbit average therefore asks exactly one equality question — is this the same unit? — and gets two answers. The tile has one value on the diagonal and one value off it.

That is why the bias strips in the RNN mosaic look like solid bars: a long diagonal line of one value, a field of the other value everywhere else. There is no room for a richer pattern because there is no free axis to carry it.

The count is the case of the Bell-number story: partitions of a one-element set are just "same" and "different", and for a pair of indices the invariant matrix is . The free-axis cases are four values and four scalars.

The two values are not independent of the other tiles. The cross moments between a bias and a weight matrix have to respect the same groups, which is why the bias strips interfere with the neighbouring tiles in the bottom-right corner of the mosaic (cross tiles).
