Global curvature is a wide-baseline secant

#curvature

A secant estimate of curvature uses a finite difference: measure how much the gradient changes over a finite step, divide by the step. The framework's step is not infinitesimal. It is the radius of the orbit — the distance from to its centroid .

That is what "global" means. The estimate is not a local Taylor approximation around ; it is an average of secant relations taken all around the orbit. In one dimension, with the variance of the parameters over the orbit and the variance of the gradient,

The baseline being wide is both the strength and the weakness. It is the strength because it makes the estimate an average over a large, symmetry-defined set rather than a noisy local probe. It is the weakness because the linearisation is only as good as the orbit's size allows, and the fidelity numbers reflect that (how good is the estimate).
