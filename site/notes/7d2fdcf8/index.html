Fifteen numbers describe any vanilla RNN block

#recurrence#algebra

The recurrent block of a vanilla RNN has fifteen numbers whether the network has 8 units or 8,000. The tile for 8 units is determined by those fifteen numbers; for 8,000 units the same fifteen numbers determine a -entry block.

The source essay's figure makes this visible in two panels. The left panel colours each entry by the equality pattern of its four indices; there are exactly fifteen colours, one per partition in (Bell numbers). The right panel shows the values themselves, clamped at so the fifteen levels are visible (red positive, blue negative). Every colour on the left is one flat level on the right.

The flatness is exact, not approximate. The largest deviation within a class came out as — not "small", zero — because the projection makes those entries bitwise identical. This is the most extreme compression the library offers, and it is available to the most classical model in the field.

The count does not depend on the size of the network, because the multiplicities are properties of the kinds, not of the dimension (the cost). The fifteen numbers are the entries of the four blocks of the isotypic decomposition.

A GRU stacks several such blocks: the GRU case.
