A commuting optimizer maps orbits to orbits

#ensemble

The ensemble claim needs one dynamical fact: the optimizer's update must commute with the group. Precisely, if is one step of the optimizer, then for every group element . Starting at and iterating is then the same as starting at and applying to every iterate.

Most optimizers do commute with a relabelling. Plain SGD does, and so do momentum, Adam, Shampoo and Muon: their update is assembled from the gradient by operations that do not care which unit is which. A permutation of the units permutes the gradient coordinates, and every elementwise or reduction operation in the update permutes with it. This is why Muon, which is not obviously permutation-invariant, is compatible with permutation symmetry at all (Muon as a corner).

What breaks the correspondence is anything that either breaks the symmetry or introduces randomness the runs do not share:

  • batchnorm's running statistics, or a per-unit learning rate (the update stops being equivariant);
  • a different data order, different dropout masks, different augmentation (the two runs see different gradients, so no group element relates them).

The distinction matters. Without loss invariance there is no orbit at all; without an equivariant optimizer there is still an orbit, but it is not the ensemble of trained runs. The construction uses both: invariance gives the level set (orbit as level set), equivariance of the optimizer gives the ensemble interpretation. The caveat is developed in the ensemble claim is narrow.
