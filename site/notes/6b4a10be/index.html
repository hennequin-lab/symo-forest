Damping is a relative eigenvalue floor

#curvature

Damping is applied as a relative eigenvalue floor. For a symmetric matrix , the damped power is

where is the largest eigenvalue (in absolute value) and is the damping constant. The floor is relative to the spectrum, so it scales with the operator rather than with the parameterization.

Two details matter in practice. A zero eigenvalue raised to a negative power is defined to be zero rather than NaN, so a degenerate orbit estimate produces a zero step instead of a crash. And the blockwise solve uses one global across all blocks, so the damping is applied consistently to the whole operator rather than block by block.

The relative form is what makes the estimate usable across layers with wildly different gradient scales: an absolute floor would be too large for one layer and too small for another. It also interacts with the rank deficiency of (why S_w is dropped): the floor is what keeps the smallest directions finite when the covariance is nearly singular.
