Does orbit averaging train?

#optimization

On an MNIST deep autoencoder and on nanoGPT, Symo's curves essentially coincide with Muon's and sit below Adam's. Given the equivalence of Muon and the signed-permutation corner, that is what you would expect, and it is a useful check of the implementation rather than a new result.

The OCaml example in the repository is a smoke test rather than a benchmark, but it runs end to end: 32 hidden units, 8 inputs, mean-squared error in 500 steps.

The important word is check. The curves coinciding is evidence that the machinery implements what it says it implements: an orbit average in the signed-permutation setting really does produce the polar-factor step. It is not evidence that orbit averaging beats Muon, because in that setting it is Muon.

That distinction is the heart of the author's assessment: the optimization results are evidence about Muon as much as about orbit averaging (why the preconditioner helps). The framework is a dial, and Muon is one setting; the intermediate ones are unexplored (the dial). The algebra, by contrast, is useful whether or not it ever replaces Adam (the durable part).
