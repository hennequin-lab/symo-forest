The centroid Hessian is a worse descent proxy

#curvature

The most surprising number in the fidelity comparison is not that the orbit estimate is crude. It is that the true Hessian at the orbit centroid is a worse proxy for the curvature along the descent direction than the averaged covariance is.

Along random directions the centroid Hessian scores roughly — worse than chance, and far below the structured estimates at to . Along it stays near zero and occasionally goes negative, while the structured estimates do better and more consistently. The local Hessian is the "correct" local object, and it loses to a wide-baseline average.

Why might that be? A plausible reading is that the descent direction is not a random direction: it is aligned with the average gradient, and the orbit-averaged covariance is built from exactly that gradient's variation across the orbit. The local Hessian at the centroid knows about infinitesimal curvature, but the optimizer takes a finite step in a direction that the orbit geometry has already selected. The wide baseline may be an advantage there, not a defect (wide-baseline secant).

This is an empirical observation, not a theorem, and the paper does not explain it. It is one of the reasons the author says the durable part of the work is the algebra rather than the optimizer (the durable part), and one of the open questions (why the preconditioner helps).
