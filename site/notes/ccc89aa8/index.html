The obvious reason is " commutes with the block decomposition". It does, but you have to apply to the blocks — the actual matrices — not to the factors — the coordinates. Those are different things, and confusing them is the classic bug. A factor list is a coordinate vector; a block is a matrix. Matrix functions act on matrices. Coordinates are what you store.

The correct reason is that the map from factor lists to operators is an isomorphism of algebras, and is defined by a power series in matrix multiplication. Algebra isomorphisms preserve multiplication, so they commute with analytic functions. That is the algebra-isomorphism argument, and it is what makes the small computation exact rather than approximate.

The distinction is developed in factors versus blocks and dramatized in the shrink warning.
