Preconditioning with orbit averages — trailhead

#meta#optimization

The application the paper is about: take a curvature estimate, invert its square root, and step against it. This trail follows the update, the two well-known optimizers that turn out to be corners of the same dial, and the parts nobody understands yet.

The step

  • Preconditioning with the inverse square root of S_g — the update and why it is a quasi-Newton step with a matrix that was never formed.
  • Muon is the signed-permutation corner of the dial — the polar factor as an inverse square root of a curvature estimate.
  • Shampoo keeps left and right factors separate — the same object with one factor per axis.
  • Everything that wants a matrix — Laplace, pruning, forgetting, influence, and what the algebra offers them.

The design space

  • The group is a hyperparameter of the approximation — the dial, and what it trades.
  • Does orbit averaging train? — the empirical evidence, and what it is evidence for.

The unknowns

  • Why the preconditioner helps is unexplained — the central gap.
  • The ablation that would change what the method is — the experiment the author most wants.
  • Comparing optimizers at matched effective step size — the confound.
  • What the EMA over factors does to the estimate — the stochastic question.
  • Which groups are good, and why — the design question.
  • The durable part is the algebra, not the optimizer — the closing assessment.

The estimate itself is the curvature trail; the computation is the scale trail. Back to the map.
