The ablation that would change what the method is

#optimization#open

The most informative experiment is cheap: remove the curvature content and see whether training changes.

Replace by a random invariant PSD operator with the same spectrum, or by a rescaled identity on each block. If training is unaffected, then what matters is invariance and scale rather than curvature — which would change what we think the method is. It would also explain why a crude estimate suffices (the theory gap) without needing a better Hessian approximation.

The experiment is cheap precisely because of the algebra: the estimator is already factorised, so producing a random invariant operator with a prescribed block spectrum is a manipulation of the small blocks, not a construction of a large matrix (the round trip). This is one of the places where the structural work pays off even if the optimizer story does not.

A null result would be interesting, not disappointing. It would say that the useful part is the projection onto relabelling-invariant directions, and that the particular curvature estimate is mostly a convenient way to choose a scale within that subspace. That is a cleaner statement than "a crude estimate suffices".
