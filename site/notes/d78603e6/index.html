The commutant algebra has three names

#ensemble#algebra

The space of matrices that commute with every element of a group acting on a vector space is called the commutant algebra of the representation, or the centralizer algebra. For a group algebra it is also called the centralizer. This forest says "the algebra of invariant matrices" and means the same object.

Its dimension is what governs the cost of everything. For one permuted axis, the commutant of the permutation action on is spanned by and — two matrices, dimension two. That is exactly why the second-order average on one axis has two free matrices, and (the δ/1 basis). For signed permutations the direction dies, leaving dimension one (one block). For two axes tied by the same group, the commutant is the partition algebra and its dimension is the Bell number (Bell numbers).

The general theorem is Schur–Weyl–Wedderburn (the general statement): the commutant is a direct sum of small matrix algebras, one per isotypic kind, and the operator is forced to be the identity on the large carrier of each kind. That is the precise sense in which "the matrix is big but the algebra is small".

The terminology tax is paid once, here. The practical consequence is that you never store the matrix; you store its coordinates in a basis of this algebra (factors versus blocks).
