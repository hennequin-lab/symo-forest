The orbit ensemble is narrower than a real ensemble

#ensemble

The group averages are exact by construction: and are computed in closed form, and the library checks them against brute-force enumeration of the group at small sizes. But the ensemble claim is narrower than it sounds.

For a real ensemble of independently trained networks, runs differ by more than relabelling: different data order, different dropout masks, different augmentation, different initialization seeds. The orbit captures the part of the spread that comes from the symmetry and nothing else. It is the conditional spread given the run, under the group, not the marginal spread over all runs.

This is not a defect of the computation; it is what the computation computes. The correct reading is: "if the only difference between two runs were a relabelling, this is their mean and covariance." Whether that is a useful model of a real ensemble is an empirical question, and it is one of the things the paper does not settle.

The narrower claim is still valuable for two reasons. Exactness is one: there is no Monte Carlo error, no sample-size choice, no variance to reduce. Interpretability is the other: the orbit covariance is a property of the loss landscape's symmetry, so it is a clean object to reason about even if it is not the whole ensemble.

The dynamic assumption behind the interpretation is the commuting optimizer; the empirical evidence about training is in does it train; the theory gap is why the preconditioner helps.
