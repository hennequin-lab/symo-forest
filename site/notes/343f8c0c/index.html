Orbit averages are invariant objects

#symmetry#ensemble

For a vector and a pair , define

where is the full Kronecker product of the group element over the tensor's axes, and the average is uniform over the group. Both averages are invariant: applying any group element to the result leaves it unchanged. That is the defining property of a projection onto the fixed points.

So an orbit average is not a statistic you have to recompute at each new point of the orbit. It is a description of the orbit, valid everywhere on it. For the ensemble interpretation this is exactly what you want: the mean weights and the mean gradient are properties of the ensemble, not of the representative you happened to train.

The invariance also explains why the averages can be computed in closed form. The average of a linear action over a group is the projection onto the trivial representation, and for permutations that projection has an elementary matrix: the uniform shadow. For the second order it is the projection onto the commutant (R2). Both are idempotent (averaging twice is averaging once).

This note is the bridge from the geometric picture (the orbit is a level set) to the ensemble construction (the ensemble you didn't have to train).
