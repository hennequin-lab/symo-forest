The orbit is a level set of the loss

#symmetry

Let be a group of orthogonal matrices acting on the parameter vector, with for every . The orbit of is

By definition, the loss is constant on it. So the orbit is contained in a level set of — a closed family of parameter vectors on which the loss takes exactly the same value.

This is stronger than "the loss is flat along some directions". The orbit need not be a manifold: a finite group gives a finite set of points, and points can be spread across the parameter space. What is flat is the loss at those points, not the loss along the straight line between them. The secant estimate of curvature uses precisely the spread of the orbit, not a tangent direction — see the wide-baseline secant.

Two consequences organize the rest of the forest. First, a single gradient is a sample of a whole family, since equivariance rotates it around the orbit. Second, averaging over the orbit produces an object the group cannot move (orbit averages are invariant), which is what makes the ensemble statistics describable once and reusable at every point of the orbit.

The centroid of the orbit is the point the group cannot move; it is defined in the orbit centroid and computed by R1.
