Why the paper sets S_w to the identity

#curvature

In practice the paper replaces by the identity and takes

the square root of the gradient covariance, as the curvature estimate. There are two stated reasons and one surprise.

The first reason is stability. is the outer product of a single displacement , so it is severely rank-deficient: its rank is at most the dimension of the orbit's displacement, which is one vector's worth. Inverting it amplifies noise along directions where it is nearly singular. The paper checks this empirically.

The second reason is cost. Dropping halves the estimation work: you no longer need to form or invert it, and the solve becomes a single symmetric square root.

The surprise is that this is not merely a numerical convenience. Empirically, tracks the true Hessian along the gradient direction better than the "correct" does. The rank-deficiency is not just a nuisance to be regularized away; the object that ignores is a better estimator for the direction that matters (the fidelity numbers).

Both estimators are structurally identical, so the rest of the forest writes for whichever of the two second-order averages is in play. The thing actually needed is a matrix function of it: to precondition, or to invert (preconditioning).
