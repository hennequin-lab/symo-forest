# Isotypic decomposition of V^⊗k under the symmetric group

Let $V = RR^n$ carry the defining permutation representation $sigma mapsto bold(P)_sigma$ of the symmetric group $cal(S)_n$. For $k in NN$, the tensor power $V^(⊗k) = RR^(n^k)$ carries the diagonal action $sigma mapsto bold(P)_sigma^(⊗k)$. Because $cal(S)_n$ is a finite group acting orthogonally, $V^(⊗k)$ decomposes into an orthogonal direct sum of **isotypic components**:
```typst
V^(⊗k) ≅ ⊕_lambda cal(S)_lambda ⊗ cal(M)_lambda,
```
where:
- $lambda$ indexes the isomorphism classes of irreducible representations of $cal(S)_n$ (Specht modules $S^([n - |lambda|, lambda])$, identified in `Kind.t` by their partition tail $lambda$ of weight $|lambda| <= k$),
- $cal(S)_lambda$ is the irreducible **carrier space** of kind $lambda$, of dimension $d_lambda (n) = dim cal(S)_lambda$, on which $cal(S)_n$ acts irreducibly, and
- $cal(M)_lambda = op("Hom")_(cal(S)_n)(cal(S)_lambda, V^(⊗k))$ is the **multiplicity space** of kind $lambda$, of width-independent dimension $m_lambda = dim cal(M)_lambda$ (the number of orthogonal copies of $cal(S)_lambda$ inside $V^(⊗k)$), on which $cal(S)_n$ acts trivially.

## Step-by-step derivation for $k = 0, 1, 2$

### 1. Case $k = 0$: $V^(⊗0) = RR$

A scalar has no permuted axes. Only the trivial representation $lambda = emptyset$ (Specht module $S^((n))$) occurs, with $d_emptyset = 1$ and multiplicity $m_emptyset = 1$.

### 2. Case $k = 1$: $V = RR^n$ ($n >= 2$)

As shown in [[d47131ad|the Π/Π⊥ projector decomposition]], the projectors $bold(Pi) = frac(1, n) bold(J)_n$ and $bold(Pi)_perp = bold(I)_n - bold(Pi)$ split $V$ into two irreducible $cal(S)_n$-submodules:
```typst
V = cal(S)_emptyset ⊕ cal(S)_([1]),
quad quad
cal(S)_emptyset = op("span")(bold(1)_n),
quad
cal(S)_([1]) = bold(1)_n^perp = { bold(x) in RR^n mid(|) sum_(i=1)^n x_i = 0 },
```
where $cal(S)_emptyset = S^((n))$ is the trivial representation ($d_emptyset = 1, m_emptyset = 1$) and $cal(S)_([1]) = S^((n-1, 1))$ is the $(n - 1)$-dimensional standard representation ($d_([1]) = n - 1, m_([1]) = 1$).

### 3. Case $k = 2$: $V^(⊗2) = V ⊗ V ≅ RR^(n times n)$ ($n >= 4$)

Substitute $V = cal(S)_emptyset ⊕ cal(S)_([1])$ into $V ⊗ V$ and expand by bilinearity:
```typst
V ⊗ V
= (cal(S)_emptyset ⊗ cal(S)_emptyset)
⊕ (cal(S)_emptyset ⊗ cal(S)_([1]))
⊕ (cal(S)_([1]) ⊗ cal(S)_emptyset)
⊕ (cal(S)_([1]) ⊗ cal(S)_([1])).
```
The first three summands are already irreducible:
- $cal(S)_emptyset ⊗ cal(S)_emptyset ≅ cal(S)_emptyset$ (1st copy of the trivial kind $emptyset$, spanned by $bold(1)_n bold(1)_n^top = bold(J)_n$),
- $cal(S)_([1]) ⊗ cal(S)_emptyset ≅ cal(S)_([1])$ (1st copy of the standard kind $[1]$, matrices $bold(x) bold(1)_n^top$ with $bold(x) in bold(1)_n^perp$),
- $cal(S)_emptyset ⊗ cal(S)_([1]) ≅ cal(S)_([1])$ (2nd copy of the standard kind $[1]$, matrices $bold(1)_n bold(x)^top$ with $bold(x) in bold(1)_n^perp$).

The fourth summand $cal(S)_([1]) ⊗ cal(S)_([1])$ (matrices in $RR^(n times n)$ whose row sums and column sums all vanish) splits into its symmetric and skew-symmetric parts:
```typst
cal(S)_([1]) ⊗ cal(S)_([1]) = op("Sym")^2 (cal(S)_([1])) ⊕ Lambda^2 (cal(S)_([1])).
```
- **The exterior square $Lambda^2(cal(S)_([1]))$** consists of skew-symmetric $n times n$ matrices with zero row and column sums. For $n >= 4$, this is the irreducible **antisymmetric** Specht module $cal(S)_([1,1]) = S^((n-2, 1, 1))$, of dimension
  ```typst
  d_([1,1])(n) = binom(n - 1, 2) = (n - 1)(n - 2) / 2,
  quad quad m_([1,1]) = 1.
  ```
- **The symmetric square $op("Sym")^2(cal(S)_([1]))$** has dimension $binom((n-1)+1, 2) = frac(n(n-1), 2)$ and decomposes for $n >= 4$ into three irreducible $cal(S)_n$-submodules:
  1. A **2nd copy of the trivial module $cal(S)_emptyset$** ($dim = 1$), spanned by the identity on $bold(1)_n^perp$, namely $bold(Pi)_perp = bold(I)_n - frac(1, n) bold(J)_n$.
  2. A **3rd copy of the standard module $cal(S)_([1])$** ($dim = n - 1$), obtained by embedding $bold(x) in bold(1)_n^perp$ along the matrix diagonal and projecting onto $bold(1)_n^perp ⊗ bold(1)_n^perp$: $op("Diag")(bold(x)) - frac(1, n)(bold(x) bold(1)_n^top + bold(1)_n bold(x)^top)$.
  3. The irreducible **symmetric-square module $cal(S)_([2]) = S^((n-2, 2))$**, consisting of symmetric matrices in $RR^(n times n)$ with *zero diagonal* and *zero row/column sums*, of dimension
     ```typst
     d_([2])(n) = n(n - 1) / 2 - 1 - (n - 1) = n(n - 3) / 2,
     quad quad m_([2]) = 1.
     ```

## Two dimension checks

In every row $k in {0, 1, 2}$, two exact identities hold:
1. **Vector-space dimension:** $sum_lambda d_lambda (n) m_lambda = dim V^(⊗k) = n^k$. For $k = 2$:
   ```typst
   1 dot 2 + (n - 1) dot 3 + n(n - 3) / 2 dot 1 + (n - 1)(n - 2) / 2 dot 1
   = (3 n - 1) + (n^2 - 3 n + 1) = n^2.
   ```
2. **Commutant algebra dimension:** By [[46af9ff5|the Wedderburn theorem]], $dim cal(C)(V^(⊗k), V^(⊗k)) = sum_lambda m_lambda^2 = B_(2k)$. For $k = 2$:
   ```typst
   sum_lambda m_lambda^2 = 2^2 + 3^2 + 1^2 + 1^2 = 4 + 9 + 1 + 1 = 15 = B_4,
   ```
   and for the symmetric subspace $cal(C)_"sym"(V^(⊗2))$:
   ```typst
   sum_lambda m_lambda (m_lambda + 1) / 2 = 3 + 6 + 1 + 1 = 11.
   ```
Notice how the $k = 2$ table also shows immediately why $n >= 4$ is required: at $n = 3$, $d_([2])(3) = 3(0)/2 = 0$, so the symmetric-square carrier $cal(S)_([2])$ disappears ([[b50cfe81|semisimplicity and the stable range]]). The explicit basis vectors for each copy in this table are constructed in [[7ee7cea7|explicit canonical test vectors]].
