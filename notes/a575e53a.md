# Product kinds across multiple groups and block assembly over parameter trees

Real neural networks have multiple independent symmetry groups $cal(G) = product_(g=1)^G cal(G)_g$ ($cal(G)_g = cal(S)_(n_g)$) and multiple parameter leaves $W = ⊕_(i=1)^L W_i$. The single-group, single-pair [[46af9ff5|Wedderburn decomposition]] extends to the full model in two algebraic steps, implemented in `Blocks.build` and `Orbit.Second_order.blocks_of_factors`.

## 1. Product kinds across multiple groups

Let leaf $i$ have $k_(i,g) in {0, 1, 2}$ axes transformed by group $cal(G)_g$ ($g = 1, dots, G$) and free space $F_i$ of dimension $f_i$, so that
```typst
W_i ≅ lr((⊗_(g=1)^G V_g^(⊗k_(i,g)))) ⊗ F_i,
quad quad V_g = RR^(n_g).
```
Substituting the single-group isotypic decomposition $V_g^(⊗k_(i,g)) ≅ ⊕_(lambda_g) cal(S)_(lambda_g)^((g)) ⊗ cal(M)_(i, lambda_g)^((g))$ into the tensor product over $g = 1, dots, G$ and distributing $⊗$ over $⊕$ yields
```typst
W_i ≅ ⊕_(bold(lambda) = (lambda_1, dots, lambda_G)) cal(S)_(bold(lambda)) ⊗ (cal(M)_(i, bold(lambda)) ⊗ F_i),
```
where:
- A **global kind** $bold(lambda) = (lambda_1, dots, lambda_G)$ (`Blocks.kind = Kind.t list`) is a $G$-tuple of per-group kinds, one for each group in the model (if group $g$ does not act on leaf $i$, i.e. $k_(i,g) = 0$, only the trivial kind $lambda_g = emptyset$ has non-zero multiplicity $m_(i, emptyset)^((g)) = 1$; `Orbit.extend` pads omitted groups with `[]`).
- The irreducible carrier of $bold(lambda)$ for the product group $cal(G) = product_g cal(S)_(n_g)$ is the outer tensor product of the per-group carriers:
  ```typst
  cal(S)_(bold(lambda)) = ⊗_(g=1)^G cal(S)_(lambda_g)^((g)),
  quad quad
  d_(bold(lambda)) = dim cal(S)_(bold(lambda)) = product_(g=1)^G d_(lambda_g)^((g)) (n_g).
  ```
- The multiplicity space of $bold(lambda)$ on leaf $i$ is the tensor product of the per-group multiplicity spaces:
  ```typst
  cal(M)_(i, bold(lambda)) = ⊗_(g=1)^G cal(M)_(i, lambda_g)^((g)),
  quad quad
  m_(i, bold(lambda)) = dim cal(M)_(i, bold(lambda)) = product_(g=1)^G m_(i, lambda_g)^((g)).
  ```
For the pair $(W_j, W_i)$, the $(i, j)$ block at global kind $bold(lambda)$ is a rectangular matrix $bold(B)_(bold(lambda))^((i, j)) in RR^((m_(i, bold(lambda)) f_i) times (m_(j, bold(lambda)) f_j))$, which is non-empty precisely when $m_(i, bold(lambda)) > 0$ and $m_(j, bold(lambda)) > 0$ (i.e. $bold(lambda)$ is a common kind of leaves $i$ and $j$).

## 2. Assembly over the parameter tree $W = ⊕_(i=1)^L W_i$

Now collect the $L$ leaves into the direct sum $W = ⊕_(i=1)^L W_i$ and group the summands by global kind $bold(lambda)$:
```typst
W = ⊕_(i=1)^L W_i
≅ ⊕_(bold(lambda)) lr([ cal(S)_(bold(lambda)) ⊗ lr((⊕_(i=1)^L cal(M)_(i, bold(lambda)) ⊗ F_i)) ]).
```
Applying [[46af9ff5|Schur's lemma]] to the direct-sum representation on $W$ shows that any global intertwiner $bold(S) in cal(C)(W, W)$ cannot couple distinct global kinds $bold(lambda) != bold(mu)$, and on each global kind $bold(lambda)$ acts as $bold(I)_(cal(S)_(bold(lambda))) ⊗ bold(B)_(bold(lambda))$, where $bold(B)_(bold(lambda))$ is a single assembled square matrix formed by tiling the per-pair blocks $bold(B)_(bold(lambda))^((i, j))$ over all active leaves $i, j$:
```typst
cal(C) lr((⊕_(i=1)^L W_i))
≅ ⊕_(bold(lambda)) op("End") lr((⊕_(i=1)^L (cal(M)_(i, bold(lambda)) ⊗ F_i))),
quad quad
bold(B)_(bold(lambda)) = lr([ bold(B)_(bold(lambda))^((i, j)) ])_(i, j = 1)^L.
```
The assembled block $bold(B)_(bold(lambda))$ has dimension
```typst
M_(bold(lambda)) times M_(bold(lambda)),
quad quad
M_(bold(lambda)) = sum_(i=1)^L m_(i, bold(lambda)) f_i,
```
which is completely independent of all permuted widths $n_1, dots, n_G$.

### Worked example: assembled block sizes for the $7$-leaf MLP-step RNN

In the [[a97e31ca|MLP-step RNN]] with two groups $cal(G)_h = cal(S)_(n_h)$ and $cal(G)_z = cal(S)_(n_z)$, every leaf has $k_(i,h), k_(i,z) in {0, 1}$, so there are $2 times 2 = 4$ global kinds $bold(lambda) = (lambda_h, lambda_z) in {emptyset, [1]} times {emptyset, [1]}$:
1. $bold(lambda) = (emptyset, emptyset)$: all $7$ leaves have $m_(i, (emptyset, emptyset)) = 1$, so $M_((emptyset, emptyset)) = 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + n_"in" + n_"out" + n_"out" = 4 + n_"in" + 2 n_"out"$.
2. $bold(lambda) = ([1], emptyset)$: only leaves with $k_(i,h) = 1$ and $k_(i,z) <= 1$ ($bold(W)_1, bold(W)_2, bold(b)_1$) contribute, giving $M_(([1], emptyset)) = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3$.
3. $bold(lambda) = (emptyset, [1])$: only leaves with $k_(i,z) = 1$ ($bold(W)_1, bold(W)_2, bold(b)_2, bold(B), bold(W)_3$) contribute, giving $M_((emptyset, [1])) = 1 + 1 + 1 + n_"in" + n_"out" = 3 + n_"in" + n_"out"$.
4. $bold(lambda) = ([1], [1])$: only leaves with both $k_(i,h) = 1$ and $k_(i,z) = 1$ ($bold(W)_1, bold(W)_2$) contribute, giving $M_(([1], [1])) = 1 + 1 = 2$.

For $n_"in" = 3, n_"out" = 2$, the entire $D times D$ operator $bold(S) = cal(R)_2(bold(u), bold(v))$ across all $7$ leaves — whether $n_h$ and $n_z$ are $8$ or $80,000$ — is isomorphic to four small matrices of sizes $11 times 11$, $3 times 3$, $8 times 8$, and $2 times 2$. In `symo`, `Orbit.Second_order.blocks_of_factors` and `factors_of_blocks` perform this exact assembly and its inverse.
