# Orthogonal representations on parameter trees

Let $W = ⊕_(i=1)^L W_i$ be the direct sum of the vectorized parameter leaves of a model, equipped with the standard Euclidean inner product $⟨bold(u), bold(v)⟩ = sum_(i=1)^L bold(u)_i^top bold(v)_i$ of total dimension $D = sum_(i=1)^L dim W_i$.

A group $cal(G)$ acts orthogonally on $W$ when its action is given by a homomorphism $rho : cal(G) -> O(W)$ into the orthogonal group of $W$:
```typst
rho(g h) = rho(g) rho(h), quad quad
rho(g)^top rho(g) = bold(I)_W <==> rho(g)^(-1) = rho(g)^top quad forall g, h in cal(G).
```
Because $cal(G)$ acts leaf by leaf, $rho(g) = ⊕_(i=1)^L rho_i (g)$ is block-diagonal across leaves. When $cal(G) = product_(a=1)^G cal(G)_a$ is a direct product of independent permutation (or signed-permutation) groups acting on distinct subsets of tensor axes, the action on leaf $i$ is the Kronecker product
```typst
rho_i (g_1, dots, g_G) = lr((⊗_(a=1)^G bold(P)_(g_a)^(⊗k_(i,a)))) ⊗ bold(I)_(F_i),
```
where $k_(i,a)$ is the number of axes of leaf $i$ transformed by group $cal(G)_a$ and $F_i$ is the space of free axes of leaf $i$. Since each permutation or signed-permutation matrix $bold(P)_(g_a)$ is orthogonal, and Kronecker products and direct sums preserve orthogonality, $rho(g)$ is orthogonal on the full parameter space $W$.

## Three structural roles of orthogonality

Restricting attention to orthogonal group actions ($rho(g)^top = rho(g)^(-1)$) is essential for three reasons:

1. **Contragredient and primal actions coincide.** Under a general invertible linear substitution $bold(w)' = bold(A) bold(w)$, a gradient transforms contragrediently as $nabla_(bold(w)') cal(L) = bold(A)^(-top) nabla_(bold(w)) cal(L)$. Orthogonality implies $bold(A)^(-top) = bold(A)$, so parameters and gradients transform under the *identical* representation $rho$ ([[6b12a533|gradient equivariance]]).
2. **Group averages are self-adjoint projections.** For any representation of a finite group, the group average $bold(Pi) = bb(E)_(g in cal(G))[rho(g)]$ is an idempotent ($bold(Pi)^2 = bold(Pi)$). It is an *orthogonal* projection ($bold(Pi)^top = bold(Pi)$) if and only if the average is self-adjoint, which follows from $rho(g)^top = rho(g^(-1))$ and the inversion invariance of the uniform measure on $cal(G)$. This holds both for the first-order average $cal(R)_1$ ([[6226b6f2|first-order projection]]) and for the second-order twirl $cal(T)$ ([[8eb163bd|second-order projection]]).
3. **Compatibility with Euclidean norms and isotropic shifts.** Because $|rho(g) bold(w)|_2 = |bold(w)|_2$, isotropic $L_2$ penalties preserve $cal(G)$-invariance ([[688fb137|objective invariance]]), and isotropic spectral shifts $bold(S) + gamma bold(I)_W$ commute with $rho(g)$ and restrict cleanly to orthonormal isotypic bases ([[93e6cfab|block functional calculus]]).
