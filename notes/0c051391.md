# Canonical test vectors and the pairing theorem

Let $W_L = V^(⊗k_L) ⊗ F_L$ and $W_R = V^(⊗k_R) ⊗ F_R$ carry the actions of $cal(G) = cal(S)_n$ in [[b50cfe81|the stable range]] $n >= 2 max(k_L, k_R)$. By [[46af9ff5|the Wedderburn theorem]], any intertwiner $bold(S) in cal(C)(W_R, W_L)$ has the canonical form
```typst
bold(S) = ⊕_lambda (bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda),
quad quad
bold(B)_lambda : cal(M)_lambda^R ⊗ F_R -> cal(M)_lambda^L ⊗ F_L.
```
We wish to extract the matrix entries of each block $bold(B)_lambda$ from the diagram expansion $bold(S) = sum_(k=1)^d bold(T)_k ⊗ bold(C)_k$ without forming the $D_L times D_R$ matrix $bold(S)$.

## Definition (canonical test vectors)

For each common isotypic kind $lambda$ occurring in $V^(⊗k_L) ≅ ⊕_lambda cal(S)_lambda^L ⊗ cal(M)_lambda^L$ and $V^(⊗k_R) ≅ ⊕_lambda cal(S)_lambda^R ⊗ cal(M)_lambda^R$:
1. Fix, once and for all, an $cal(S)_n$-module identification $cal(S)_lambda^L ≅ cal(S)_lambda^R ≅ cal(S)_lambda$ and a single unit **internal vector** $bold(e)_lambda in cal(S)_lambda$ ($|bold(e)_lambda|_2 = 1$).
2. Choose bases ${bold(m)_1^L, dots, bold(m)_(m_lambda^L)^L}$ of $cal(M)_lambda^L$ and ${bold(m)_1^R, dots, bold(m)_(m_lambda^R)^R}$ of $cal(M)_lambda^R$ (called the **copies** of kind $lambda$).

The **canonical test vectors** on the permuted factors $V^(⊗k_L)$ and $V^(⊗k_R)$ are the embedded vectors
```typst
bold(z)_(lambda a)^L = bold(e)_lambda ⊗ bold(m)_a^L in V^(⊗k_L) quad (a = 1, dots, m_lambda^L),
quad quad
bold(z)_(lambda b)^R = bold(e)_lambda ⊗ bold(m)_b^R in V^(⊗k_R) quad (b = 1, dots, m_lambda^R),
```
normalized so that $|bold(z)_(lambda a)^L|_2 = |bold(z)_(lambda b)^R|_2 = 1$ (equivalently $|bold(m)_a^L|_2 = |bold(m)_b^R|_2 = 1$). Note the two structural requirements in this definition:
- **The same internal vector $bold(e)_lambda$** is used on the left and on the right; and
- **The same internal vector $bold(e)_lambda$** is used for every copy $a = 1, dots, m_lambda$ of kind $lambda$.

## Theorem (the pairing theorem)

Let $bold(S) = ⊕_nu (bold(I)_(cal(S)_nu) ⊗ bold(B)_nu) in cal(C)(V^(⊗k_R), V^(⊗k_L))$ (for simplicity with $f_L = f_R = 1$; for general free spaces $F_L, F_R$, each pairing is an $f_L times f_R$ matrix slice of $bold(B)_lambda$). Then for any kinds $lambda, mu$ and copies $a in {1, dots, m_lambda^L}$, $b in {1, dots, m_mu^R}$,
```typst
⟨bold(z)_(lambda a)^L, bold(S) bold(z)_(mu b)^R⟩ = delta_(lambda mu) (bold(G)_lambda^L bold(B)_lambda)_(a b),
```
where $bold(G)_lambda^L in RR^(m_lambda^L times m_lambda^L)$ is the Gram matrix of the left copy basis:
```typst
(bold(G)_lambda^L)_(a a') = ⟨bold(m)_a^L, bold(m)_(a')^L⟩ = ⟨bold(z)_(lambda a)^L, bold(z)_(lambda a')^L⟩.
```
In particular, when the copy basis ${bold(m)_a^L}$ is orthonormal ($bold(G)_lambda^L = bold(I)_(m_lambda^L)$), the inner product *is* the block entry:
```typst
⟨bold(z)_(lambda a)^L, bold(S) bold(z)_(lambda b)^R⟩ = (bold(B)_lambda)_(a b).
```

### Proof

Because distinct isotypic components $cal(S)_lambda ⊗ cal(M)_lambda$ and $cal(S)_mu ⊗ cal(M)_mu$ ($lambda != mu$) of an orthogonal representation are mutually orthogonal, and $bold(S)$ maps the $mu$-isotypic component of $V^(⊗k_R)$ into the $mu$-isotypic component of $V^(⊗k_L)$, we have $⟨bold(z)_(lambda a)^L, bold(S) bold(z)_(mu b)^R⟩ = 0$ whenever $lambda != mu$.

When $lambda = mu$, substituting $bold(S)|_(cal(S)_lambda ⊗ cal(M)_lambda^R) = bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda$ and expanding $bold(B)_lambda bold(m)_b^R = sum_(a'=1)^(m_lambda^L) (bold(B)_lambda)_(a' b) bold(m)_(a')^L$ gives
```typst
⟨bold(z)_(lambda a)^L, bold(S) bold(z)_(lambda b)^R⟩
= ⟨bold(e)_lambda ⊗ bold(m)_a^L, (bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda)(bold(e)_lambda ⊗ bold(m)_b^R)⟩
= ⟨bold(e)_lambda, bold(e)_lambda⟩ dot ⟨bold(m)_a^L, bold(B)_lambda bold(m)_b^R⟩
= 1 dot sum_(a'=1)^(m_lambda^L) ⟨bold(m)_a^L, bold(m)_(a')^L⟩ (bold(B)_lambda)_(a' b)
= (bold(G)_lambda^L bold(B)_lambda)_(a b),
```
where $⟨bold(e)_lambda, bold(e)_lambda⟩ = 1$ because both $bold(z)_(lambda a)^L$ and $bold(z)_(lambda b)^R$ use the *same* unit internal vector $bold(e)_lambda in cal(S)_lambda$. $square$

## Why one test vector per copy suffices

Because $bold(S)$ acts as $bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda)$ on the entire $d_lambda(n)$-dimensional carrier $cal(S)_lambda$, a *single* unit vector $bold(e)_lambda in cal(S)_lambda$ witnesses the action of $bold(B)_lambda$ on the whole carrier at once. The number of test vectors required on a side of order $k$ is therefore the sum of the multiplicities, not the ambient dimension $n^k$:
```typst
N_"test" (k) = sum_lambda m_lambda = cases(
  1 & "for" k = 0,
  1 + 1 = 2 & "for" k = 1,
  2 + 3 + 1 + 1 = 7 & "for" k = 2,
)
```
yielding $sum_lambda m_lambda^2 = 1, 2, 15$ pairings respectively. The concrete construction of these $2$ and $7$ test vectors is given in [[7ee7cea7|explicit canonical test vectors for $k = 1$ and $k = 2$]], and how the pairings are precomputed once at compile time is explained in [[f914d5ad|the compile-time block dictionary]].
