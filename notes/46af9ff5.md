# Schur's lemma and the Wedderburn decomposition of the commutant

Let $W_L = V^(⊗k_L) ⊗ F_L$ and $W_R = V^(⊗k_R) ⊗ F_R$ be two parameter leaf spaces carrying the actions $rho_L(sigma) = bold(P)_sigma^(⊗k_L) ⊗ bold(I)_(F_L)$ and $rho_R(sigma) = bold(P)_sigma^(⊗k_R) ⊗ bold(I)_(F_R)$ of $cal(G) = cal(S)_n$, with free dimensions $f_L = dim F_L$ and $f_R = dim F_R$.

By [[35cc480f|the isotypic decomposition of $V^(⊗k)$]], because $cal(S)_n$ acts trivially on $F_L$ and $F_R$, the isotypic decompositions of $W_L$ and $W_R$ are
```typst
W_L ≅ ⊕_lambda cal(S)_lambda ⊗ (cal(M)_lambda^L ⊗ F_L),
quad quad
W_R ≅ ⊕_lambda cal(S)_lambda ⊗ (cal(M)_lambda^R ⊗ F_R),
```
where $dim cal(S)_lambda = d_lambda(n)$ and $dim(cal(M)_lambda^(L,R) ⊗ F_(L,R)) = m_lambda^(L,R) f_(L,R)$.

## Theorem (Wedderburn block decomposition of the commutant)

Let $n >= 2 max(k_L, k_R)$ lie in [[b50cfe81|the stable range]]. Then:
1. Every intertwiner $bold(S) in cal(C)(W_R, W_L) = {bold(S) : W_R -> W_L mid(|) bold(S) rho_R(sigma) = rho_L(sigma) bold(S) quad forall sigma in cal(S)_n}$ has the unique block-diagonal decomposition
   ```typst
   bold(S) = ⊕_lambda (bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda),
   ```
   where $lambda$ runs over the common kinds occurring in both $V^(⊗k_L)$ and $V^(⊗k_R)$, $bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda)$ is the $d_lambda(n) times d_lambda(n)$ identity operator on the carrier $cal(S)_lambda$, and
   ```typst
   bold(B)_lambda : cal(M)_lambda^R ⊗ F_R -> cal(M)_lambda^L ⊗ F_L
   ```
   is a matrix of size $(m_lambda^L f_L) times (m_lambda^R f_R)$, called the **Wedderburn block** of $bold(S)$ at kind $lambda$.
2. The linear map $(bold(B)_lambda)_lambda mapsto bold(S) = ⊕_lambda (bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda)$ is a vector-space isomorphism
   ```typst
   ⊕_lambda RR^((m_lambda^L f_L) times (m_lambda^R f_R)) tilde.equiv cal(C)(W_R, W_L),
   quad quad
   dim cal(C)(W_R, W_L) = sum_lambda m_lambda^L m_lambda^R f_L f_R.
   ```
3. When $W_L = W_R = W$ ($k_L = k_R = k, f_L = f_R = f$), this map is an **isomorphism of $RR$-algebras** with involution (transpose): for any $bold(S), bold(S)' in cal(C)(W, W)$ with blocks $(bold(B)_lambda), (bold(B)'_lambda)$,
   ```typst
   bold(S) bold(S)' = ⊕_lambda (bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ (bold(B)_lambda bold(B)'_lambda)),
   quad quad
   bold(S)^top = ⊕_lambda (bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda^top).
   ```

### Proof

1. **Sufficiency.** On the $lambda$-isotypic summand $cal(S)_lambda ⊗ (cal(M)_lambda ⊗ F)$, $rho(sigma)$ acts as $rho_lambda(sigma) ⊗ bold(I)_(cal(M)_lambda ⊗ F)$. Therefore, for any matrices $bold(B)_lambda$,
   ```typst
   (bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda)(rho_lambda (sigma) ⊗ bold(I)_(cal(M)_lambda^R ⊗ F_R))
   = rho_lambda (sigma) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda
   = (rho_lambda (sigma) ⊗ bold(I)_(cal(M)_lambda^L ⊗ F_L))(bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda),
   ```
   so $⊕_lambda (bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda) in cal(C)(W_R, W_L)$.
2. **Necessity (Schur's lemma).** Let $bold(S) in cal(C)(W_R, W_L)$ and consider its restriction-projection $bold(S)_(mu, lambda) : cal(S)_lambda ⊗ (cal(M)_lambda^R ⊗ F_R) -> cal(S)_mu ⊗ (cal(M)_mu^L ⊗ F_L)$ between kinds $lambda$ and $mu$. Because the Specht modules $cal(S)_lambda$ over $RR$ are absolutely irreducible (every $cal(S)_n$-endomorphism of $cal(S)_lambda$ is a scalar multiple of $bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda)$), Schur's lemma implies:
   - If $mu != lambda$, $op("Hom")_(cal(S)_n)(cal(S)_lambda, cal(S)_mu) = {0}$, so $bold(S)_(mu, lambda) = bold(0)$: distinct kinds do not interact.
   - If $mu = lambda$, $op("Hom")_(cal(S)_n)(cal(S)_lambda ⊗ (cal(M)_lambda^R ⊗ F_R), cal(S)_lambda ⊗ (cal(M)_lambda^L ⊗ F_L)) = op("Hom")_(cal(S)_n)(cal(S)_lambda, cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ op("Hom")(cal(M)_lambda^R ⊗ F_R, cal(M)_lambda^L ⊗ F_L) = RR bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ RR^((m_lambda^L f_L) times (m_lambda^R f_R))$, so $bold(S)_(lambda, lambda) = bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda$ for a unique matrix $bold(B)_lambda$.
3. **Algebra homomorphism.** When $W_L = W_R$ and the isotypic decomposition is taken in an orthonormal basis, $(bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda)(bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)'_lambda) = bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ (bold(B)_lambda bold(B)'_lambda)$ and $(bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda)^top = bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda^top$ by the mixed-product and transpose properties of the Kronecker product. $square$

## Structural consequence: width-independent complexity

In the decomposition $bold(S) = ⊕_lambda (bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda) ⊗ bold(B)_lambda)$:
- The **carrier** $cal(S)_lambda$ has dimension $d_lambda(n)$ growing as $O(n^k)$, and accounts for the huge dimension $D = n^k f$ of the ambient matrix $bold(S)$. Because $bold(S)$ is forced by symmetry to act as the identity $bold(I)_(cal(S)_lambda)$ on $cal(S)_lambda$, the carrier holds *zero degrees of freedom*.
- All free parameters of $bold(S)$ reside in the **Wedderburn blocks** $bold(B)_lambda$, whose dimensions $(m_lambda^L f_L) times (m_lambda^R f_R)$ depend only on the kind multiplicities $m_lambda$ ($<= 3$ for $k <= 2$) and the free dimensions $f_L, f_R$, **never on the permuted width $n$**.

Consequently, storing $bold(S)$ costs $sum_lambda m_lambda^L m_lambda^R f_L f_R$ numbers independent of $n$, and diagonalizing or inverting $bold(S)$ in the square case ($k_L = k_R = k, f_L = f_R = f$) reduces from $O((n^k f)^3)$ to $sum_lambda O((m_lambda f)^3)$ ([[93e6cfab|block functional calculus]]).
