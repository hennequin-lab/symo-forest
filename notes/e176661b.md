# Projecting outer products via compile-time Cholesky and einsums

Let ${bold(T)_1, dots, bold(T)_d}$ be the compiled basis of diagram components spanning the commutant on the permuted axes of a leaf pair $(W_R, W_L)$, where $W_L = V^(⊗k_L) ⊗ F_L$ ($dim F_L = f_L$) and $W_R = V^(⊗k_R) ⊗ F_R$ ($dim F_R = f_R$). Any operator $bold(S) in cal(C)(W_R, W_L)$ has a unique expansion
```typst
bold(S) = sum_(k=1)^d bold(T)_k ⊗ bold(C)_k,
```
where the coefficients $bold(C)_1, dots, bold(C)_d in RR^(f_L times f_R)$ are the **factors** of $bold(S)$.

## Proposition (normal equations for the exact commutant projection)

Let $bold(X) in RR^(D_L times D_R)$ be any matrix (in particular, a rank-one outer product $bold(X) = bold(u) bold(v)^top$ with $bold(u) in W_L, bold(v) in W_R$). The factors $bold(C)_1, dots, bold(C)_d in RR^(f_L times f_R)$ of its twirl projection $cal(T)(bold(X)) in cal(C)(W_R, W_L)$ (and thus of $cal(R)_2(bold(u), bold(v)) = cal(T)(bold(u) bold(v)^top)$) are the unique solution of the linear normal equations
```typst
sum_(l=1)^d bold(G)_(k l) bold(C)_l = bold(B)_k quad (k = 1, dots, d),
```
where $bold(G) in RR^(d times d)$ is the [[b81e35a1|symbolic Gram matrix]] $bold(G)_(k l) = ⟨bold(T)_k, bold(T)_l⟩_F$ on the permuted axes, and $bold(B)_k in RR^(f_L times f_R)$ is the contraction of $bold(X)$ against $bold(T)_k$ over the permuted axes:
```typst
(bold(B)_k)_(alpha beta) = sum_(bold(i), bold(j)) bold(T)_k [bold(i), bold(j)] X_((bold(i), alpha), (bold(j), beta)).
```

### Proof

By [[8eb163bd|the twirl projection theorem]], $cal(T)(bold(X))$ is the Frobenius-orthogonal projection of $bold(X)$ onto $cal(C)(W_R, W_L) = op("span"){bold(T)_k ⊗ bold(E)_(alpha beta)}$, where ${bold(E)_(alpha beta)}$ is the standard orthonormal basis of $RR^(f_L times f_R)$. Orthogonality of the residual $bold(X) - cal(T)(bold(X))$ to every basis element $bold(T)_k ⊗ bold(E)_(alpha beta)$ gives
```typst
⟨bold(T)_k ⊗ bold(E)_(alpha beta), sum_(l=1)^d bold(T)_l ⊗ bold(C)_l⟩_F
= ⟨bold(T)_k ⊗ bold(E)_(alpha beta), bold(X)⟩_F.
```
Since $⟨bold(T)_k ⊗ bold(E)_(alpha beta), bold(T)_l ⊗ bold(C)_l⟩_F = ⟨bold(T)_k, bold(T)_l⟩_F (bold(C)_l)_(alpha beta) = bold(G)_(k l) (bold(C)_l)_(alpha beta)$, the free indices decouple completely from the $d times d$ Gram matrix $bold(G)$, yielding the stated normal equations $bold(G) bold(C) = bold(B)$ for all $f_L f_R$ free-axis entries simultaneously. $square$

## Compile-time factorization and $O(D)$ runtime contraction

Two structural properties make solving $bold(G) bold(C) = bold(B)$ fast at scale:

1. **Compile-time Cholesky factorization of $bold(G)$.** The $d times d$ Gram matrix $bold(G)$ (where $d = 2$ for $k=1$, $d = 11$ or $15$ for $k=2$) depends only on the symmetry specification and the permuted dimensions $n$, never on the runtime vectors $bold(u), bold(v)$. In `Compiler.build_estimator`, $bold(G)$ is assembled symbolically at compile time and factored once in `float64` via Cholesky decomposition $bold(G) = bold(L) bold(L)^top$ (with a $10^(-8) frac(op("tr")(bold(G)), d) bold(I)_d$ jittered retry and an eigendecomposition pseudoinverse fallback if the basis is degenerate). At runtime, `Compiler.estimate_factors` stacks $bold(B)_1, dots, bold(B)_d$ into a matrix of shape $d times (f_L f_R)$ and executes two triangular solves (`solve_triangular`) against $bold(L)$ and $bold(L)^top$ in $O(d^2 f_L f_R)$ operations.
2. **Factorized einsum contraction for $bold(X) = bold(u) bold(v)^top$.** Forming the outer product $bold(u) bold(v)^top in RR^(D_L times D_R)$ would require $O(D_L D_R)$ memory. Because $bold(T)_k$ is a product of Kronecker deltas on the tie blocks $B in P_k$, `Term.coefficient` compiles the contraction $bold(B)_k = ⟨bold(T)_k, bold(u) bold(v)^top⟩$ directly on the pair `` `Outer_product (u, v) `` as a two-operand einsum: every tie block $B$ assigns a single shared einsum character to all axes in $B$, untied permuted axes receive distinct characters omitted from the output string (so they are summed out), and only the free axes $F_L, F_R$ appear in the output string ([[edc05432|einsum compilation of terms]]). The cost of computing $bold(B)_k$ from $(bold(u), bold(v))$ is therefore $O(D_L + D_R + f_L f_R)$, linear in the parameter count $D$.
