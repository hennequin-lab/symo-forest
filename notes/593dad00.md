# Finite and continuous weight-space symmetry groups

Neural architectures admit several natural families of weight-space symmetries. Distinguishing the finite orthogonal groups from continuous or non-orthogonal groups clarifies which symmetries yield closed-form orthogonal projections in the partition algebra.

| symmetry family | group $cal(G)$ | group order $|cal(G)|$ | orthogonal in raw weights? | commutant algebra on $V^(⊗k)$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| hidden units, channels, or heads | symmetric group $cal(S)_n$ | $n!$ | yes | partition algebra $P_k (n)$ (dim $B_(2k)$) |
| odd activation ($phi(-z) = -phi(z)$) | hyperoctahedral group $cal(B)_n = {plus.minus 1}^n times.r cal(S)_n$ | $2^n n!$ | yes | even-block partition subalgebra |
| multi-layer / multi-axis products | direct product $product_(a=1)^G cal(S)_(n_a)$ | $product_(a=1)^G n_a !$ | yes | tensor product $⊗_(a=1)^G P_(k_a)(n_a)$ |
| linear bottleneck ($phi(z) = z$) | orthogonal group $O(n)$ | $oo$ (compact Lie) | yes | Brauer algebra $B_k (n)$ |
| homogeneous activation ($"ReLU"$) | positive rescaling $(RR_+)^n$ | $oo$ (non-compact) | no | requires gauge fixing |

## Finite permutation and signed-permutation groups

The first three rows are finite subgroups of $O(W)$ generated by coordinate permutations and sign flips:
- **Single-group permutation ($cal(S)_n$).** Relabelling $n$ hidden units in an MLP layer, $n$ convolutional feature maps, $n$ attention heads, or $n$ recurrent state units ([[b2ecabc3|hidden-unit permutations]], [[c35dde47|vanilla RNN conjugation]]).
- **Signed permutations ($cal(B)_n$).** When the nonlinearity is odd ($tanh$, linear, or odd polynomial), negating a unit's incoming and outgoing weights simultaneously leaves the network map unchanged, enlarging $cal(S)_n$ to $cal(B)_n$ ([[2addfd8f|signed permutations]]).
- **Direct products ($product_a cal(G)_a$).** Independent layers or independent internal/state axes in recurrent cells ([[a97e31ca|crossing groups in an MLP-step RNN]]) carry direct product groups whose representations tensor across the group factors ([[a575e53a|product kinds]]).

## Continuous groups: $O(n)$ and positive rescaling

In a purely linear layer $bold(y) = bold(W)_2 bold(W)_1 bold(x)$, any orthogonal matrix $bold(Q) in O(n)$ satisfies $(bold(W)_2 bold(Q)^top)(bold(Q) bold(W)_1) = bold(W)_2 bold(W)_1$. Because $O(n)$ is a compact Lie group acting orthogonally, Haar-measure orbit averages remain orthogonal projections, and the commutant on $V^(⊗k)$ is the *Brauer algebra* (spanned by pair-matchings rather than arbitrary set partitions).

By contrast, for a positively $1$-homogeneous activation such as $"ReLU"$, where $phi(c z) = c phi(z)$ for all $c > 0$, scaling row $i$ of $bold(W)_1$ (and $b_(1,i)$) by $c_i > 0$ and column $i$ of $bold(W)_2$ by $c_i^(-1)$ preserves the network function. However, the resulting group $(RR_+)^n$ is non-compact (so no uniform probability measure exists on the orbit) and acts by diagonal matrices $"diag"(bold(c)) ⊕ "diag"(bold(c))^(-1)$ that are *not* orthogonal for $c_i != 1$. Consequently, the Euclidean projection formulas of [[588b9727|orthogonal representations]] apply to the finite orthogonal subgroups $cal(S)_n$ and $cal(B)_n$ (and compact orthogonal groups), rather than to non-compact rescalings.
