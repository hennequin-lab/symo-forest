# The twirl and second-order orbit average project onto the commutant

Let $W_L$ and $W_R$ be two real Euclidean spaces of dimensions $D_L$ and $D_R$ carrying [[588b9727|orthogonal representations]] $rho_L : cal(G) -> O(W_L)$ and $rho_R : cal(G) -> O(W_R)$ of a finite group $cal(G)$ (for instance, two parameter leaves $W_i, W_j$ of a network, or the full parameter space $W_L = W_R = W$).

Equip the space of matrices $RR^(D_L times D_R)$ with the Frobenius inner product
```typst
⟨bold(A), bold(B)⟩_F = op("tr")(bold(A)^top bold(B)) = sum_(alpha=1)^(D_L) sum_(beta=1)^(D_R) A_(alpha beta) B_(alpha beta).
```
The **commutant** (or space of **intertwiners**) of the pair $(W_R, W_L)$ is the linear subspace of matrices that intertwine the two representations:
```typst
cal(C)(W_R, W_L) = { bold(S) in RR^(D_L times D_R) mid(|) bold(S) rho_R (g) = rho_L (g) bold(S) quad forall g in cal(G) }.
```
Since $rho_R (g)$ is orthogonal ($rho_R (g)^(-1) = rho_R (g)^top$), the intertwining condition $bold(S) rho_R (g) = rho_L (g) bold(S)$ is equivalently written as fixed-point invariance under conjugation:
```typst
rho_L (g) bold(S) rho_R (g)^top = bold(S) quad forall g in cal(G).
```
When $W_L = W_R = W$ and $rho_L = rho_R = rho$, $cal(C)(W, W)$ is closed under matrix multiplication and transposition, forming a symmetric matrix algebra: the **commutant algebra** of $(W, rho)$.

## Definition (twirl and second-order orbit average)

The **twirl** of a matrix $bold(X) in RR^(D_L times D_R)$ is the uniform group average
```typst
cal(T)(bold(X)) = bb(E)_(g in cal(G)) [ rho_L (g) bold(X) rho_R (g)^top ].
```
For vectors $bold(u) in W_L$ and $bold(v) in W_R$, the **second-order orbit average** is the twirl of their rank-one outer product $bold(u) bold(v)^top$:
```typst
cal(R)_2 (bold(u), bold(v)) = cal(T)(bold(u) bold(v)^top) = bb(E)_(g in cal(G)) [ (rho_L (g) bold(u)) (rho_R (g) bold(v))^top ] in cal(C)(W_R, W_L).
```

## Proposition (orthogonal projection onto the commutant)

1. $cal(T) : RR^(D_L times D_R) -> RR^(D_L times D_R)$ is the Frobenius-orthogonal projection onto $cal(C)(W_R, W_L)$.
2. For any $bold(u) in W_L, bold(v) in W_R$, $cal(R)_2(bold(u), bold(v))$ is the unique best approximation to $bold(u) bold(v)^top$ in $cal(C)(W_R, W_L)$ in Frobenius norm:
   ```typst
   cal(R)_2 (bold(u), bold(v)) = arg min_(bold(S) in cal(C)(W_R, W_L)) | bold(u) bold(v)^top - bold(S) |_F^2.
   ```
3. $cal(T)$ and $cal(R)_2$ are idempotent ($cal(T)(cal(T)(bold(X))) = cal(T)(bold(X))$) and orbit-invariant: for any $h in cal(G)$, $cal(R)_2(rho_L (h) bold(u), rho_R (h) bold(v)) = cal(R)_2(bold(u), bold(v))$.
4. On a diagonal pair ($W_L = W_R = W$, $rho_L = rho_R = rho$), for any $bold(u) in W$, the matrix $cal(R)_2(bold(u), bold(u))$ is symmetric and positive semidefinite ($cal(R)_2(bold(u), bold(u)) >= bold(0)$).

### Proof

**Step 1 (Image lies in $cal(C)$).** For any $h in cal(G)$,
```typst
cal(T)(bold(X)) rho_R (h) = bb(E)_(g in cal(G)) [ rho_L (g) bold(X) rho_R (g^(-1) h) ].
```
Substituting $g' = h^(-1) g$ (so $g = h g'$ and $g^(-1) h = (g')^(-1)$) and using left-invariance of the uniform expectation on $cal(G)$ gives
```typst
cal(T)(bold(X)) rho_R (h) = bb(E)_(g' in cal(G)) [ rho_L (h) rho_L (g') bold(X) rho_R (g')^top ] = rho_L (h) cal(T)(bold(X)).
```
Thus $cal(T)(bold(X)) in cal(C)(W_R, W_L)$.

**Step 2 (Fixes $cal(C)$).** If $bold(S) in cal(C)(W_R, W_L)$, then $rho_L (g) bold(S) rho_R (g)^top = bold(S) rho_R (g) rho_R (g)^top = bold(S)$ for every $g in cal(G)$ by orthogonality of $rho_R (g)$, so $cal(T)(bold(S)) = bold(S)$. In particular, $cal(T)^2 = cal(T)$.

**Step 3 (Self-adjointness in the Frobenius inner product).** For any $bold(A), bold(B) in RR^(D_L times D_R)$, cyclicity of the trace and orthogonality $rho(g)^top = rho(g^(-1))$ give
```typst
⟨bold(A), cal(T)(bold(B))⟩_F
= bb(E)_(g in cal(G)) op("tr") lr((bold(A)^top rho_L (g) bold(B) rho_R (g)^top))
= bb(E)_(g in cal(G)) op("tr") lr(((rho_L (g)^top bold(A) rho_R (g))^top bold(B)))
= ⟨cal(T)(bold(A)), bold(B)⟩_F,
```
where the last equality reindexes $g mapsto g^(-1)$. Since $cal(T)$ is an idempotent self-adjoint operator on $(RR^(D_L times D_R), ⟨dot, dot⟩_F)$ with image $cal(C)(W_R, W_L)$, it is the orthogonal projection onto $cal(C)(W_R, W_L)$, proving (1), (2), and (3).

**Step 4 (Positive semidefiniteness).** When $W_L = W_R = W$ and $bold(u) = bold(v)$, each summand $(rho(g) bold(u))(rho(g) bold(u))^top$ is symmetric positive semidefinite: for every test vector $bold(x) in W$,
```typst
bold(x)^top cal(R)_2 (bold(u), bold(u)) bold(x) = bb(E)_(g in cal(G)) [ ⟨bold(x), rho(g) bold(u)⟩^2 ] >= 0.
```
Hence $cal(R)_2(bold(u), bold(u))$ is symmetric positive semidefinite. $square$

## Significance

Because $cal(R)_2(bold(u), bold(v))$ is the orthogonal projection of $bold(u) bold(v)^top$ onto $cal(C)(W_R, W_L)$, computing the exact orbit average over all $|cal(G)|$ group elements reduces to finding the coordinates of $bold(u) bold(v)^top$'s orthogonal projection in a basis of $cal(C)(W_R, W_L)$ ([[e176661b|compile-time projection solve]]). Moreover, because $cal(C)(W, W)$ is a linear subspace closed under matrix algebra operations, any convex combination or average of orbit averages $sum_s w_s cal(R)_2(bold(u)_s, bold(u)_s)$ with $w_s >= 0$ remains an exact positive semidefinite element of $cal(C)(W, W)$, and its spectral functions remain inside $cal(C)(W, W)$ ([[93e6cfab|block functional calculus]]).
